HEAT · TRANSIENT01

Heating a cold slab from one face

Given: Initially uniform slab, left face at 650 K, right face insulated.

  1. Conservationaccumulation = net conduction in
  2. Local balanceρcₚ ∂T/∂t = −dqₓ/dx
  3. Fourier lawqₓ = −k dT/dx
  4. Governing equation∂T/∂t = α ∂²T/∂x²
PredictionHeat penetrates from the left; total energy rises by the net boundary heat input.
HEAT · COMPOSITE02

Two materials carrying the same heat flux

Given: A low-conductivity layer is placed at the centre of the slab.

  1. Steady conservationdqₓ/dx = 0
  2. Flux continuityqₓ,1 = qₓ,2
  3. Fourier lawdT/dx = −qₓ/k
  4. Consequencelower k ⇒ larger |dT/dx|
Key distinctionThe global energy balance sees one heat rate; the field reveals where the temperature drop occurs.
MASS · DIFFUSION03

Solute entering a diffusion couple

Given: Solute concentration is imposed on the left of an initially solute-free layer.

  1. Species balance∂cₐ/∂t = −dJₐ,x/dx
  2. Fick lawJₐ,x = −Dₐ dcₐ/dx
  3. Constant Dₐ∂cₐ/∂t = Dₐ ∂²cₐ/∂x²
  4. Time scaletᴅ ~ L²/Dₐ
PredictionIncreasing diffusivity broadens the concentration profile and shortens homogenisation time.
MOMENTUM · COUETTE04

Momentum diffusion from a moving wall

Given: Flow is in x; velocity ux varies only across y.

  1. Momentum balanceρ ∂uₓ/∂t = dτₓᵧ/dy
  2. Newtonian stressτₓᵧ = μ duₓ/dy
  3. Momentum fluxJₚₓ,ᵧ = −μ duₓ/dy = −τₓᵧ
  4. Steady solutionuₓ(y) is linear
Sign conventionShear stress follows the shear rate; molecular momentum flux points down the velocity gradient.
