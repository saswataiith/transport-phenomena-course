HEAT · TRANSIENT01

Heating a cold slab from one face

A plane wall of thickness L = 20 mm is initially cold. At t = 0 its left face is suddenly brought to 650 K while the right face is insulated.

Domain
0 ≤ x ≤ L; one-dimensional plane wall
Initial condition
T(x,0) = 300 K
Boundary conditions
T(0,t) = 650 K;  ∂T/∂x(L,t) = 0
Properties
k = 9.57 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹; ρcp = 3.0 MJ m⁻³ K⁻¹
Assumptions
Constant properties; no advection; no internal heat generation
  1. Conservationaccumulation = net conduction in
  2. Local balanceρcₚ ∂T/∂t = −dqₓ/dx
  3. Fourier lawqₓ = −k dT/dx
  4. Governing equation∂T/∂t = α ∂²T/∂x²
  5. Closureα = k/(ρcₚ)
Prediction and checkHeat penetrates from the left over a scale √(αt). The temperature remains between 300 K and 650 K, and the rise in stored energy must equal the integrated heat entering at x = 0.
HEAT · COMPOSITE02

Two materials carrying the same heat flux

A 20 mm composite wall contains a 4 mm low-conductivity central layer. Its two faces are maintained at different temperatures until steady state is reached.

Domain
0 ≤ x ≤ 20 mm; interfaces at x = 8 and 12 mm
Initial condition
Not needed for the final steady solution; use T(x,0) = 300 K to watch the transient approach
Boundary conditions
T(0) = 675 K; T(L) = 300 K
Properties
Outer layers: k; central layer: 0.12k
Interface conditions
T is continuous and qx is continuous
  1. Steady conservationdqₓ/dx = 0
  2. Flux continuityqₓ,1 = qₓ,2
  3. Fourier lawdT/dx = −qₓ/k
  4. Consequencelower k ⇒ larger |dT/dx|
Key distinctionThe global energy balance sees one constant heat rate. The resolved field shows the steepest temperature gradient—and therefore most of the temperature drop—inside the low-k layer.
MASS · DIFFUSION03

Solute diffusing into a layer

A solute-free layer of thickness L = 2 mm is exposed at its left face to a maintained solute concentration; the right face is impermeable.

Domain
0 ≤ x ≤ 2 mm
Initial condition
cA(x,0) = 0 mol m⁻³
Boundary conditions
cA(0,t) = 850 mol m⁻³; ∂cA/∂x(L,t) = 0
Properties
DA = 10⁻¹⁰ m² s⁻¹
Assumptions
Constant DA; no bulk flow; no homogeneous reaction
  1. Species balance∂cₐ/∂t = −dJₐ,x/dx
  2. Fick lawJₐ,x = −Dₐ dcₐ/dx
  3. Constant Dₐ∂cₐ/∂t = Dₐ ∂²cₐ/∂x²
  4. Time scaletᴅ ~ L²/Dₐ
Prediction and checkThe penetration distance grows as √(DAt), while the diffusion time scales as L²/DA. Concentration must remain between 0 and 850 mol m⁻³.
MOMENTUM · COUETTE04

Momentum diffusion from a moving wall

An incompressible Newtonian fluid occupies a 10 mm gap. At t = 0 the lower wall begins moving in the x direction while the upper wall remains fixed.

Domain
0 ≤ y ≤ H = 10 mm; velocity vx(y,t)
Initial condition
vx(y,0) = 0
Boundary conditions
vx(0,t) = Vw = 1.4 m s⁻¹; vx(H,t) = 0
Properties
ρ = 1000 kg m⁻³; μ = 0.0316 Pa s
Assumptions
Incompressible; no pressure gradient; no slip; vy = 0
  1. Continuity∂vx/∂x + ∂vy/∂y = 0 → 0 = 0
  2. Momentum balanceρ ∂vx/∂t = −dJyx(p)/dy
  3. Momentum-flux lawJyx(p) = −μ dvx/dy
  4. Index meaningy direction; x-momentum
  5. Governing equation∂vₓ/∂t = ν ∂²vₓ/∂y²
  6. Steady solutionvₓ(y) = Vw(1 − y/H)
Sign convention and checkJyx(p) is the flux of x-momentum in the y direction and points down the velocity gradient. Momentum penetrates over √(νt); the final profile is linear.
